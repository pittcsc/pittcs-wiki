> This is a part of a series of guides called Zero to Offer aimed to help you navigate the internship process. We recommend you start [here](/guides/zero-to-offer/)!

Internships at top tech companies are sought after for good reason. You get to meet interesting students from all over the world, receive competitive salaries, experience amazing mentorship, and be pampered with perks from cool companies. So you might be wondering: How do I even get to work at one of these companies?

Luckily for you, this guide will tell you what to expect and how to prepare at a high level.

## Introduction

### Commitment

Getting these internships requires a decent amount of effort. How badly do you want to intern at a "top" company?

- How much work are you willing to invest into your career?
- Do you really want to be a software engineer?

As college students, we are at a meaningful stage of our lives. While you should focus on your career and your academics, you should also have fun, make friends, try out new things, and figure out what you like and dislike. Landing a top internship takes a lot of work, but it is extremely rewarding and sets you up for a lot of success. That being said, **it is possible to have fun in college and simultaneously grind through the internship hunt**.

### Perception versus Reality

Society has ingrained preconceptions of prestige and brand, and it is easy to assume a big-name company will automatically make you happier than a smaller or local one. In reality, your day-to-day growth depends way more on your team, mentorship, projects, and location than the logo on your hoodie, and you don't need a FAANG badge to have a great summer. We unpack prestige, money, and happiness more deeply in **[Mindset](/guides/zero-to-offer/mindset)**.

Ultimately, the most important advice is to _apply everywhere_. Apply early and apply everywhere, and assess your options after you get an offer. Apply even if you do not meet the requirements for a company.

## The Internship Process

The internship process is actually pretty simple. **You get an interview, and then you pass it.**

### Getting an Interview

Getting an interview is contingent on a few things:

- Having foundational skills
- A strong resume
- Applying early and at scale

We dive deep into the first piece in the [Building Your Case](/guides/zero-to-offer/building-your-case) section and cover resumes in [Resumes & ATS](/guides/zero-to-offer/resume).

#### The Numbers Game & AEAP

In a normal recruiting cycle, cold-applying online feels like throwing PDFs into a void. Expect to get ghosted or auto-rejected by the vast majority of companies, even strong candidates do. That is why volume and timing matter:

- **The 500-Application Math:** Even if your per-application offer rate is a brutal 1%, applying to **500 companies** gives you a **>99% chance** (`1 - 0.99^500`) of landing at least one offer. PittCSC officer Liam Brem applied to **327 internships as a freshman** before landing Meta.
- **Set a daily pace:** Aim for **~5 applications a day (150/month)** rather than binge-applying once in October.
- **Apply Within 24 Hours (AEAP: As Early As Possible):** Many companies review rolling applications first-come, first-served and close portals once their interview queue fills. Applying **within a day of a role opening** dramatically improves your odds of a human actually seeing your resume.

To move fast without burning out on Workday accounts:

1. **Bookmark the PittCSC & Simplify Internships GitHub repo** (search *"PittCSC internships repo"*): it is updated daily during peak season.
2. **Install [Simplify Copilot](https://simplify.jobs/)** so repetitive Workday forms take under a minute instead of ten.
3. **Keep your resume PDF saved on your phone** so you can fire off an application the second a posting drops.
4. **Track everything in a simple spreadsheet** (company, role, date applied, OA/interview status) so follow-ups and deadlines don't slip.

#### Ghosting

A company "ghosts" a candidate when they ignore the candidate's application. This practice is extremely common among tech companies since the number of applications they receive is so large. Getting ghosted isn't a reflection of your incapability. Even the strongest candidates get ghosted, so just keep applying!

#### Company Timelines

Every company is different and will have a different application timeline. Knowing which companies are hiring at certain parts of the year will help you tailor your applications to specific companies.

**Note:** These descriptions are all generalizations.

**Quant / Trading (May–July):** Proprietary trading firms and hedge funds open SWE and Quant internship applications earliest, often late spring through mid-summer. **If you like math, finance, or fast-paced systems, check out our [guide on Trading companies](/guides/career/trading).**

**Group A:** Companies whose main export is software and their main labor force is software people. Group A companies (think Google, Meta, Robinhood) open as early as July–August and have most positions filled by November, though many hire into the next year.

**Group B:** Companies who cannot ship products without the use of software. Group B companies (Apple, NetApp, Cisco) will generally have their positions filled by December/January.

**Group C:** Companies who use software as a support tool, but their primary export is not software development. Group C companies (FedEx, Dick's Sporting Goods, UPMC, PNC) hire well into the spring.

Most hiring begins in the fall (August, September, October) and by January only a few companies are still hiring at the same scale. This is why you have to **apply as early as possible.**

#### Off-Season & Non-Traditional Paths

Don't box yourself into "Summer SWE at Big Tech or bust":

- **Fall & Spring Internships / Co-ops:** Off-season internships and co-ops have way less applicant volume than summer cycles. Taking a semester off school for a strong internship or co-op is a great way to break into the industry and get varied experience.
- **Think Outside Pure SWE:** IT, data science, security, and business analyst internships are still real industry experience, many Pitt students parlayed a freshman IT or analyst role into SWE offers the following year.
- **Startups & Underclassmen Programs:** Check out our **[Working at a Startup](/guides/career/working-at-startup)** and **[Freshman & Sophomore Opportunities](/guides/career/freshman-sophomore-opportunities)** guides for paths with lower resume-screen friction.

#### Final Thoughts

- We can't emphasize this enough - apply as early as possible!!
- Getting the interview is the toughest part, so don't worry if your initial response is weak.
- Applying online is tough, time consuming, and kind of like shooting into a black hole. Nevertheless, it's completely worth it because it allows you to easily apply to many companies!
- Keep an open mind in terms of the companies you apply to, especially when you are looking for your first internship. Beggars can't be choosers!
- Companies are mean to freshmen, tepid to sophomores, and kind of nice to juniors.
- A large amount of luck is involved (more on this in the next section).

### Passing the Interview

Once you manage to get an interview, you have to pass it to land the offer. This entails:

- Understanding the concepts of Data Structures and Algorithms (`CS 0445`, `CS 1501`)
- Studying [LeetCode](http://leetcode.com/) and NeetCode 150
- Behavioral Interviews
- Having Strong Communication Skills

These steps are covered extensively in **[Behavioral Interviews](/guides/zero-to-offer/behavioral-interviews)** and **[Technical Interviews & OAs](/guides/zero-to-offer/technical-interviews)**.

#### Interview Timeline

A typical modern interview pipeline looks like this:

1. **Apply AEAP** online (or via career fair / referral).
2. **Online Assessment (OA):** Either an **Auto-OA** sent to everyone (CodeSignal, HackerRank, HireVue, or decision games) or a **Screened OA** sent after a recruiter reviews your resume.
3. **Recruiter / Technical Phone Screen:** A 15–30 minute recruiter vibe check or a 45-minute live coding screen with an engineer.
4. **Virtual Superday / Final Round:** 2–4 back-to-back behavioral and technical rounds over video (in-person onsites are rare outside select quant firms and final rounds).
5. **Offer / Team Matching:** You hear back 1–3 weeks later or faster if you have a competing offer deadline. Some companies (like Google) have a team-matching phase after passing interviews.

All of this interviewing ends up being a decent amount of work! To successfully land an internship, you need to do a lot of interviews. **Treat interviewing like a 3 credit class**.
