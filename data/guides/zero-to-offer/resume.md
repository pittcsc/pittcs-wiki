Having a strong resume won't pass an interview for you, but it **is** the skeleton key that gets you in the room. Before you spend 50 hours grinding LeetCode, make sure your resume can actually survive an application portal.

## You Are a Piece of Paper (For About 7 Seconds)

When you submit an online application, recruiters don't see your potential, your personality, or how hard you worked on your last project: they see a PDF. That PDF has to survive two filters:

1. **The ATS (Applicant Tracking System):** Software like Greenhouse, Workday, Rippling, Bullhorn, BambooHR, JazzHR, or Breezy parses your PDF, extracts text, and checks how well your skills and experience match the job description. If you use a multi-column Canva template, tables, or decorative fonts, the parser can mangle your text before a human ever sees it.
2. **The 7-Second Human Skim:** If you pass the filter, a recruiter or hiring manager spends roughly **7 seconds** scanning your page to decide whether you belong in the "interview" pile.

## Before You Format Anything Else

- **Use a battle-tested template:** Grab **[Jake's Resume on Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/jakes-resume/syzfjbzwjncs)**. Your custom two-column design is almost certainly hurting your ATS parse rate. Stick to **1 page, 1 column, black and white, regular font, PDF only**: no photos, no tables, no weird bullet symbols, and never hide invisible white keyword text.
- **Run it through [VMock Pitt](https://www.vmock.com/pitt):** Pitt gives every student **10 free ATS-style resume scans per year**. Use them before you start mass-applying.
- **Post in PittCSC Discord `#resume-reviews`:** Upload **both the PDF and a screenshot/image** so officers and upperclassmen can read it directly on mobile without downloading ten files.
- **Save your resume PDF on your phone:** Many roles review applications rolling first-come, first-served. Having your PDF ready on mobile lets you apply within hours of a posting dropping.

## Write Bullets With the Google XYZ Formula

Weak bullets list job duties (*"Worked with a team on backend APIs"*). Strong bullets follow the **Google XYZ formula**: **Accomplished `[X]` as measured by `[Y]` by doing `[Z]`**.

- **Weak:** *"Helped build caching for a microservice at Prudential."*
- **Strong (XYZ):** *"Decreased database queries by 25% for one microservice by implementing caching using AWS Elasticache."*
- **Strong (XYZ):** *"Developed tooling for an internal customer management system, resulting in a 15% decrease in cumulative service time."*

Keep these bullet rules in mind:

- **Lead with strong action verbs:** Use **Built, Developed, Implemented, Optimized, Reduced, Led**. Avoid passive openers like **Assisted, Helped, Worked with**, or **Collaborated** (unless you quantify your exact contribution).
- **Include both abbreviations and full terms:** Write *"AI (artificial intelligence)"* or *"ML (machine learning)"* where natural so ATS keyword searches catch both.
- **Order everything by impact:** Put your strongest bullets, strongest projects, and most relevant skills first so a 7-second skim hits your best work immediately.

## Section-by-Section Quick Rules

- **Contact:** Use a personal email (not `.edu` if you're graduating soon), clickable shortened links (GitHub, LinkedIn, personal site), and optional phone, city, or citizenship/clearance if relevant.
- **Education:** Format cleanly as `BS Computer Science, University of Pittsburgh` with your expected graduation date, GPA if **`>= 3.5`**, short-form relevant coursework, and student org leadership (PittCSC, SteelHacks, etc.).
- **Experience vs. Projects:** List experience in reverse-chronological order with **1–3 bullets** per role (present tense for current roles, past tense for finished ones). Put the **company name first, then your title** underneath. If you don't have tech experience yet, move **Projects above** non-tech jobs.
- **Projects:** Include personal, team, hackathon, and open-source projects, and **skip dates on projects** as well as basic tutorial or class-assignment clones. You must be able to explain every project on your page in depth.
- **Skills:** Group them cleanly (Languages, Frameworks/Libraries, Tools like Git, Docker, or Postman). Never use `4/5` skill bars or progress ratings, and never list a tool you can't defend in a technical interview.
- **Content Don'ts:** Drop high school activities and awards ASAP (*"like a hot potato"*), don't write like a LinkedIn post, spell-check everything, and never lie about metrics or tools.
- **Maintain Track-Specific Resumes:** If you're applying to specialized roles alongside general SWE (like Game Dev, PM, Security, or AI/ML), keep tailored versions ready instead of sending one generalist resume everywhere.

## Keep a Brag Sheet & Networking Rolodex

Updating your resume at the start of every fall is painful if you forget what you shipped six months ago. Keep two simple running docs:

- **Brag Sheet:** Log metrics, shipped features, start/end dates, courses, and project details as they happen.
- **Networking Rolodex:** Track people you meet (name, where you met, what you talked about) so follow-ups are natural and specific.
- **Google yourself:** Make sure your public LinkedIn, GitHub, and personal site match the dates and stories on your resume.
